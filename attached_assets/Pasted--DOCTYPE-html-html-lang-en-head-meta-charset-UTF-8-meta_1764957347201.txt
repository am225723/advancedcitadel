                    <h1>A Note to Eric</h1>
                    
                    <p>Eric, I know your story. You’ve been through it all. CBT since you were 15, EMDR, exposure therapy, talk therapy, meds stacked on meds, even ketamine. You’ve done every worksheet, every exercise, every version of ‘try harder.’ And honestly? You’ve earned the right to roll your eyes when someone suggests ‘therapy’ again. Because from your perspective, you’ve already test-driven every model on the lot — and all of them stalled out. So why would this be different?</p>

                    <p>Here’s my answer: because I’m not offering you the same thing you’ve already done. I’m not another therapist with a clipboard and a smile asking, ‘How does that make you feel?’ I’m not going to bury you under another pile of worksheets you’ve seen 48 different times. You don’t need that. You don’t need another lecture or another failed promise. You need someone who will get in the trenches with you. And that’s where I come in.</p>

                    <p><strong>I’m alternative. Way outside the box.</strong></p>

                    <p>I’ll be the guy who builds Legos with you at the park because sometimes your brain needs something to do with your hands, not another “deep breathing worksheet.” I’ll ghost with you to check out some random diner, or backroads, or whatever place sparks your curiosity — so your nervous system gets reminded that life still has things worth showing up for. I’ll sit elbows-deep with you under the hood of your Evo, making fun of how it’s basically a tin can while also pointing out that the way you hold that car together with duct tape and spare parts isn’t so different from how you’ve been holding yourself together.</p>

                    <p>I’ll even show up at your place, controller in hand, and kick your ass in whatever video game you want — because therapy doesn’t have to mean two chairs and a box of tissues. Sometimes it means laughing, competing, hanging out, and letting real conversations happen while we’re doing something else.</p>

                    <p>And here’s the promise: I’ll still be your therapist. I’ll still push you, still challenge you, still call you out when you’re running from yourself. But I’ll also be the best friend in the room — the one you think of when you’re spiraling, when you’re doubting yourself, when you just need someone to sit nearby so you’re not alone with your thoughts. And when that moment comes, you’ll know without question: I’ll be there.</p>

                    <p>You don’t need another therapist who looks at you like a case study. You need someone who meets you where you are, in your real life — who can talk cars, games, panic attacks, and self-doubt all in the same breath, and actually mean it.</p>

                    <p>And yeah, I’ll still use tools — but not in the boring, copy-paste way you’ve seen before. They’ll feel like tuning your Evo so it can actually handle the road instead of breaking down at every turn. That might mean cognitive reframing — like adjusting your alignment, so your thoughts don’t keep pulling you into the same old ditches, giving you straighter lines to drive. Or nervous system regulation — the equivalent of keeping your engine cool, so you don’t overheat every time panic slams the gas, which lets you go longer without burning out. Sometimes it’ll be exposure — but on your terms — testing the brakes and pushing the corners gradually, until you can trust both yourself and the car to handle more speed without spinning out. Other times it might be parts work or narrative therapy — like popping the hood, looking at the mismatched parts, and realizing the story you’ve been telling yourself about being “broken” is just wiring that can be rewired, giving you more horsepower than you thought you had.</p>

                    <p>Each of these tools works because they aren’t about forcing you into someone else’s vehicle — they’re about making your Evo roadworthy. And when the car runs better, so does the driver. The destination? Feeling confident behind the wheel instead of white-knuckling it. Trusting yourself to take the roads you’ve been avoiding. And maybe, finally, having moments where the ride feels free, fun, and actually worth staying in.</p>

                    <p>Right now, you keep telling yourself, ‘I’ll figure it out.’ But you’ve also admitted, ‘I haven’t figured it out.’ That’s the stuck point. Therapy with me isn’t about me handing you the map. It’s about sitting shotgun in your Evo — not grabbing the wheel, not barking directions, but helping you notice when you slam the brakes, when you drift into panic curves, when you floor it just to feel alive — and then figuring out how you actually want to handle those roads.</p>

                    <p>Because right now, you’re surviving. White-knuckling it through panic, trying not to lose everything, telling yourself you’ll tough it out. That’s not living. That’s barely hanging on. And I think you deserve more.</p>

                    <p>You’ve been stuck test-driving clunky old sedans of therapy that don’t handle your road. I’m not offering another sedan. I’m offering to ride shotgun in the Evo — tin can or not — and help you make the ride something you actually want to stay in.</p>

                    <p>So yeah, I’ll be the therapist. But I’ll also be the guy who builds, explores, wrenches, and laughs with you — and proves you’re not alone, every damn time. If you’re done stalling out in other people’s sedans, let’s take your Evo on the road you actually want to drive.</p>

                    <h2>About Psychotherapy Services</h2>
                    <p>Psychotherapy is a working cooperative relationship between you and your therapist. Each member of this cooperative relationship has certain responsibilities. Your therapist will contribute their knowledge, expertise, and clinical skills. You, as the client, have the responsibility to bring an attitude of collaboration and a commitment to the therapeutic process. While there are no guarantees regarding the outcome of the treatment, your commitment may increase the likelihood of a satisfactory experience.</p>
                    <p>As a client in psychotherapy, you have certain rights and responsibilities that are important for you to understand. There are also legal limitations to those rights that you should be aware of. I, as your therapist, have corresponding responsibilities to you. These rights and responsibilities are described in the following sections.</p>
                    <p><strong>Please note that psychotherapy is not an emergency service. If you are experiencing suicidal or homicidal thoughts, are in crisis, or need immediate help, please call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department.</strong></p>

                    <h2>Benefits and Risks of Psychotherapy</h2>
                    <p>Psychotherapy has both benefits and risks. Risks may include experiencing uncomfortable feelings, such as sadness, guilt, anxiety, anger, frustration, loneliness and helplessness, because the process of psychotherapy often requires discussing the unpleasant aspects of your life. However, psychotherapy has been shown to have benefits for individuals who undertake it. Therapy often leads to a significant reduction in feelings of distress, increased satisfaction in interpersonal relationships, greater personal awareness and insight, increased skills for managing stress and resolutions to specific problems.</p>
                    <p>Note that there are no guarantees about what will happen. Psychotherapy requires a very active effort on your part. In order to be most successful, you will have to work on things we discuss outside of sessions.</p>

                    <h2>The First Few Sessions</h2>
                    <p>The first few sessions typically involve a comprehensive evaluation of your needs. By the end of the evaluation, I will be able to offer you some initial impressions of what our work might include. At that point, we will discuss your treatment goals and create an initial treatment plan. You should evaluate this information and make your own assessment about whether you feel comfortable working with me. If you have questions about my procedures, we should discuss them whenever they arise. If your doubts persist, I will be happy to help you set up a meeting with another mental health professional for a second opinion.</p>

    <script>
        document.addEventListener('DOMContentLoaded', function() {
            
            const pageFlip = new St.PageFlip(document.getElementById('book'), {
                width: 450, 
                height: 600,
                size: 'stretch',
                minWidth: 315,
                maxWidth: 1000,
                minHeight: 420,
                maxHeight: 1350,
                maxShadowOpacity: 0.5, 
                showCover: true,
                mobileScrollSupport: false
            });

            pageFlip.loadFromHTML(document.querySelectorAll('.page'));

            // Prevent Page Flip when interacting with Inputs
            const inputs = document.querySelectorAll('.no-flip, input, textarea, label, a');
            inputs.forEach(input => {
                input.addEventListener('mousedown', (e) => e.stopPropagation());
                input.addEventListener('touchstart', (e) => e.stopPropagation());
                input.addEventListener('click', (e) => e.stopPropagation());
            });

            // Signature Pad Logic
            const canvases = document.querySelectorAll('canvas.signature-pad');
            
            canvases.forEach(canvas => {
                const ctx = canvas.getContext('2d');
                let isDrawing = false;
                let lastX = 0;
                let lastY = 0;

                // Wet Ink Style
                ctx.strokeStyle = "#00008B"; // Dark blue ink
                ctx.lineWidth = 2.5; // Thicker stroke
                ctx.lineCap = "round";
                ctx.lineJoin = "round";
                ctx.shadowBlur = 1; // Subtle bleed effect
                ctx.shadowColor = "#00008B";

                function getPos(e) {
                    const rect = canvas.getBoundingClientRect();
                    const clientX = e.clientX || e.touches[0].clientX;
                    const clientY = e.clientY || e.touches[0].clientY;
                    return {
                        x: (clientX - rect.left) * (canvas.width / rect.width),
                        y: (clientY - rect.top) * (canvas.height / rect.height)
                    };
                }

                function startDraw(e) {
                    e.stopPropagation(); 
                    isDrawing = true;
                    const pos = getPos(e);
                    lastX = pos.x;
                    lastY = pos.y;
                }

                function draw(e) {
                    if (!isDrawing) return;
                    e.preventDefault(); 
                    e.stopPropagation(); 
                    
                    const pos = getPos(e);
                    ctx.beginPath();
                    ctx.moveTo(lastX, lastY);
                    
                    // Bezier curve for smoother lines
                    // Using simple lineTo for now, but with round caps/joins it looks decent
                    ctx.lineTo(pos.x, pos.y);
                    ctx.stroke();
                    
                    lastX = pos.x;
                    lastY = pos.y;
                }

                function stopDraw(e) {
                    isDrawing = false;
                }

                canvas.addEventListener('mousedown', startDraw);
                canvas.addEventListener('mousemove', draw);
                canvas.addEventListener('mouseup', stopDraw);
                canvas.addEventListener('mouseout', stopDraw);

                canvas.addEventListener('touchstart', startDraw, {passive: false});
                canvas.addEventListener('touchmove', draw, {passive: false});
                canvas.addEventListener('touchend', stopDraw);
            });

            // Clear Signature Buttons
            const clearBtns = document.querySelectorAll('.clear-sig-btn');
            clearBtns.forEach(btn => {
                btn.addEventListener('click', (e) => {
                    e.stopPropagation();
                    const canvas = e.target.previousElementSibling;
                    const ctx = canvas.getContext('2d');
                    ctx.clearRect(0, 0, canvas.width, canvas.height);
                });
            });

            // Navigation Controls
            document.getElementById('prevBtn').addEventListener('click', () => pageFlip.flipPrev());
            document.getElementById('nextBtn').addEventListener('click', () => pageFlip.flipNext());

        });
    </script>
